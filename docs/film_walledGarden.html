Logline

When Steve, a maintenance worker in a vast botanical conservatory, begins seeing shimmering parallel earth overlays, he encounters Grim — a heat‑bearing presence that exists across realities. As the walls of Steve’s inner garden dissolve, he must decide whether to step beyond the life he knows.

Executive Summary

The Walled Garden is a 30‑minute pilot that combines intimate character drama with a new visual language: Gaussian splat volumetric imagery. The pilot functions as both a proof of concept for a serialized mythic sci‑fi and a festival‑ready short. This document unifies the creative vision, production plan, technical pipeline, budget tiers, and distribution strategy into a single production bible for internal review and pitching.

Pilot Episode

Tone and Visual Approach

Intimate, mythic, and experimental. The pilot is designed to be experienced as a projected Gaussian splat film: point clouds, volumetric silhouettes, and dissolving geometry that emphasize mood and internal states over literal realism.

Main Character

Steve — 34, maintenance worker at a botanical conservatory. Quiet, observant, carrying a sense of misfit longing. His arc in the pilot: enclosed → aware → destabilized → awakened.

Presence

Grim — a recurring silhouette that exists across parallel earths. Hot inside, cold outside. A witness and mirror rather than a guide. Appears as layered splats on ridges and peaks.

Concept frame: Steve inside the walled garden rendered as Gaussian splats

Visual Language

The film is designed to be experienced as a volumetric projection. Key visual rules:

  • Environments and characters are represented as Gaussian splats and point clouds.
  • Transitions between parallel earths are achieved by crossfading splat layers and shifting anisotropy.
  • Color and temperature signal emotional state: cool desaturation for containment, warm cores for internal pressure.
  • Sound design is integral: low frequency pulses for heatline, thin wind textures for peaks, close intimate Foley for the garden.

Grim

AI Coordinator Pipeline Training to Render

Pipeline Stages

  1. Data Ingestion — import footage, sync cameras, normalize exposure, extract frames.
  2. Pre Processing — camera pose estimation, depth estimation, segmentation.
  3. Training — optimize point positions, opacity, color, anisotropy; multi‑GPU or cloud compute.
  4. Scene Orchestration — align parallel earths, blend transitions, manage camera paths and lighting.
  5. Rendering — splat rendering with DOF, motion blur, color grade, export masters.
  6. Post Processing — sound design, score, dialogue sync, final mastering.

Capture Requirements Camera Count and Frame Acquisition

How Many Cameras

  • Single Camera — minimum. Walkaround capture for static scenes. Lower cost, more cleanup.
  • Dual Camera — recommended minimum for characters. Reduces occlusion and improves depth.
  • Multi Camera Array — 6–12 cameras for high fidelity volumetric capture in a single moment. Best for closeups and complex motion.
  • Environment Capture — roaming single or two cameras for large spaces; focus on coverage and overlap.

How to Capture a Frame of Data

A frame of data is a bundle: image pixels, camera pose, depth cues, and metadata. Follow these steps:

  1. Position the camera — maintain consistent distance and smooth motion; use a gimbal.
  2. Record high resolution — 4K or 6K preferred; lock exposure and white balance.
  3. Maintain overlap — ensure each frame shares features with previous frames for robust tracking.
  4. Capture multiple angles — front, sides, back for characters; high and low passes for environments.
  5. Extract frames — AI coordinator extracts frames at intervals (every 3rd–5th frame) for training.
  6. Generate camera poses — use COLMAP or Nerfstudio to compute pose graphs for each frame.
  7. Feed into training — frames become points for color, opacity, depth, and anisotropy optimization.

Result: a dense Gaussian splat reconstruction ready for cinematic rendering and layer blending.
