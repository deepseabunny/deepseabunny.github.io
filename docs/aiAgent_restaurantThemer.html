Concept overview

The AI Coordinator is a central agent that orchestrates a network of specialized agents and human decision-makers to plan and execute restaurant makeovers and theme changes.

It supports:

  • Theme shifts: country-based, holiday-based, or seasonal concepts.
  • Street takeovers: partial or full street closures with city cooperation.
  • Operational necessities: permits, safety, staffing, vendors, and guest experience.
The AI Coordinator never acts alone on high-impact decisions. It proposes, simulates, and documents options, while humans approve, override, or refine each critical step.

Core roles and responsibilities

Human roles

  • Restaurant Owner / GM: Defines goals, budget, and risk tolerance; approves final plans.
  • Creative Director / Brand Lead: Curates themes, visual identity, and guest experience.
  • Operations Lead: Confirms staffing, service flow, and safety constraints.
  • City Liaison / Legal Counsel: Reviews permits, regulations, and city coordination plans.

AI agents

  • AI Coordinator (central): Orchestrates all agents, tracks state, and manages approvals.
  • Theme & Experience Agent: Generates theme concepts, mood boards (text), and experience flows.
  • Menu & Beverage Agent: Proposes menu changes aligned with theme, cost, and kitchen capacity.
  • City & Compliance Agent: Drafts permit checklists, city requests, and safety requirements.
  • Street Takeover Logistics Agent: Plans street layout, vendor placement, crowd flow, and timing.
  • Budget & Vendor Agent: Estimates costs, compares vendors, and highlights trade-offs.
  • Communication & Marketing Agent: Drafts announcements, social posts, and neighbor notices.

In implementation, each agent can be a separate service or prompt profile, with the AI Coordinator acting as the router and state manager.

End-to-end workflow

Human-in-the-loop checkpoints
1. Intent capture & constraints
Owner / GM + AI Coordinator

The owner describes the desired makeover: e.g., “3-night Tokyo Street Festival in July, with partial street closure and outdoor seating.”

Human: goals, budget, dates AI: normalize requirements
2. Theme exploration & options
Theme & Experience Agent

The agent generates multiple theme options (countries, holidays, seasonal concepts) with pros/cons and operational notes.

AI: generate 3–5 options Human: select or merge options
3. Operational feasibility pass
Operations + City & Compliance Agents

Agents simulate capacity, staffing, and regulatory needs, then flag conflicts (noise ordinances, fire codes, alcohol rules).

AI: constraints & risks Human: confirm what’s realistic
4. Street takeover planning
Street Logistics + City & Compliance Agents

For street events, agents propose layouts, closure windows, emergency access, and city coordination steps.

AI: draft layouts & timelines Human: adjust for local reality
5. Budget, vendors, and trade-offs
Budget & Vendor Agent

The system estimates costs and offers trade-offs (e.g., fewer live performers vs. more decor; local vendors vs. imports).

AI: scenarios & ranges Human: choose scenario
6. Final plan & approvals
AI Coordinator + All stakeholders

The AI Coordinator compiles a single, human-readable plan: timelines, responsibilities, city requests, and communication templates.

Human: sign-off required Risk: no auto-approval
7. Execution support & live adjustments
AI Coordinator + On-site team

During the event, the AI Coordinator tracks checklists, timing, and incident logs, suggesting adjustments that humans can accept or reject.

AI: suggestions & alerts Human: final decisions

Human-in-the-loop design

Mandatory approval gates
  • Theme selection: AI proposes; humans choose.
  • Street closure & city requests: AI drafts; humans review and send.
  • Budget commitments: AI estimates; humans approve spend.
  • Safety & compliance: AI flags; humans validate with local rules.
What AI is not allowed to do
  • Sign contracts or legal documents.
  • Confirm permits or licenses as “approved.”
  • Override human decisions or city regulations.
  • Make staffing or firing decisions.

Approval state model

Item State Who can change it
Theme concept Draft → Proposed → Approved Human only
City request packet Draft → Reviewed → Sent Human only
Budget scenario Draft → Recommended → Approved Human only
Live adjustments Suggested → Accepted / Rejected Human only

City cooperation & street takeovers

City-facing interactions are always human-sent

Key necessities addressed by agents

  • Permits: street closure, noise, alcohol, food handling, fire safety.
  • Neighbors: notification letters, quiet hours, access guarantees.
  • Emergency access: clear lanes, signage, and coordination with authorities.
  • Insurance & liability: coverage checks and documentation reminders.

Example: city request packet (AI-drafted, human-sent)

Subject: Request for Temporary Street Closure - "Lisbon Night Market" Event To: [City Events Department Contact] Summary: - Restaurant: Casa do Sol - Proposed dates: Sept 12–14, 6pm–11pm - Location: 400–500 block of Oak Street - Purpose: Themed "Lisbon Night Market" with food stalls, live Fado music, and outdoor seating. Key points: - Emergency lane maintained at 12 ft width along east side of street. - Maximum capacity: 350 guests, based on fire code estimates. - Noise levels: Live music to end by 10pm; recorded music at reduced volume until 11pm. - Security: 2 licensed security staff + 1 off-duty officer (pending approval). - Waste & cleanup: Vendor contract includes nightly cleanup and recycling. Attachments: - Proposed street layout (PDF) - Insurance certificate - Neighbor notification draft [Prepared by City_Compliance_Agent, reviewed and sent by Restaurant Owner]

Theme examples

Scenario A: Country-based takeover
“Tokyo Street Festival” Weekend
  • Visuals: lanterns, alley-style signage, minimalist neon.
  • Menu: yakitori, ramen flight, highball bar, non-alcoholic tea pairings.
  • Street: food stalls, gacha corner, photo alley.
  • City: noise and crowd management, clear emergency lane.
Scenario B: Holiday theme
“Diwali Lights & Spice” Pop-Up
  • Visuals: warm lighting, rangoli patterns, marigold-inspired decor.
  • Menu: regional thalis, chaat station, mithai tasting flight.
  • Street: lamp installations, safe candle alternatives, family zones.
  • City: fire safety focus, crowd density limits, accessible pathways.

Safety, ethics, and boundaries

  • Respect local law: AI suggestions are advisory; humans must verify with local authorities.
  • Noise & neighbors: prioritize community impact and clear communication.
  • Accessibility: ensure layouts support mobility, vision, and hearing needs.
  • Worker well-being: avoid schedules or concepts that overburden staff.
The AI Coordinator is a planning and documentation partner, not a replacement for local expertise, legal review, or lived experience.
