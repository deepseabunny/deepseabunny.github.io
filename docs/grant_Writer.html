Uniform-Integrated Vehicle Access System
Research & Prototype Project

Applicant (Fictitious): Phoenix Civic Innovation Lab (a 501(c)(3) partner of the City of Phoenix)

Sponsoring Government Entity: City of Phoenix, Arizona

Tribal Gaming Grant Recipients: Ak-Chin Indian Community & Tohono O'odham Nation

1. Project Summary

Phoenix Civic Innovation Lab, in partnership with the City of Phoenix, seeks funding from the Ak-Chin Indian Community and the Tohono O'odham Nation to design, test, and evaluate a Uniform-Integrated Vehicle Access System. In this project, a secure chip embedded in city employee uniforms serves as the “key” to municipal vehicles, enabling identity-bound access, reducing key loss, and improving public safety and operational efficiency.

2. Statement of Need

The City’s current fleet access relies on physical keys and fobs, which are vulnerable to loss, theft, and misuse. Rekeying vehicles is costly, tracking key custody is labor-intensive, and unauthorized use is difficult to prevent. These challenges affect public safety, service reliability, and taxpayer resources. A modern, secure, wearable access solution is needed to ensure that only authorized personnel can operate city vehicles and that usage is fully auditable.

3. Project Description

3.1 Concept

The project will embed encrypted RFID/NFC chips into city uniforms. When an authorized employee approaches a retrofitted city vehicle, the uniform communicates with a vehicle-mounted reader to authenticate the user and enable unlocking and ignition. Each access event is logged, creating a secure, identity-bound record of vehicle use.

3.2 Phases

  • Phase 1 – Research & Feasibility: Technical, cybersecurity, and durability analysis.
  • Phase 2 – Prototype: 50–100 chip-embedded uniforms and 10–20 retrofitted vehicles.
  • Phase 3 – Pilot: 90-day deployment in one city department (e.g., Public Works).
  • Phase 4 – Evaluation: Final report, cost model, and citywide rollout plan.

3.3 Goals & Outcomes

  • Reduce key-related incidents and unauthorized vehicle use.
  • Improve response times and operational readiness for field staff.
  • Increase accountability through detailed access logs.
  • Deliver a validated prototype that can scale across departments.

3.4 Community Benefit

Phoenix residents benefit from safer, more reliable city services, reduced vehicle misuse, and lower costs associated with key replacement and downtime. The project also demonstrates a replicable innovation model that can be shared with regional partners and other municipalities.
